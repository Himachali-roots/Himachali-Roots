A note from the founder

This began with
my daadi.

Himachal Pradesh is more than mountains. It is memory. It is a story still being told.

Many forces passed through these hills, each leaving their mark. But through all of it, something held: our culture, our traditions, our language, carried not in books but in voices, songs and stories.

That is what I grew up not knowing the value of.
  1. 01

    I didn’t know what I had

    Every winter I visited my daadi in Mandi. I couldn’t fully understand what she said, and I didn’t try to. When you are young, culture just feels like background, like the mountains.

  2. 02

    Distance made it clear

    At 15 I moved away. Slowly I started missing it: the people, the winters, the way my daadi’s house smelled, the sounds of a language I had never bothered to learn.

  3. 03

    Now I listen

    When my daadi tells me stories of her childhood, I am not just learning about her. I am learning about who I am and where I come from.

My father always said that a person with no connection to their roots cannot truly grow. I was 15 when he said it and I rolled my eyes. I am 23 now and I understand exactly what he meant.

Himachali Roots is my attempt to change that: a digital platform to preserve and share Himachali languages, folk songs, ballads, village stories and oral traditions, not as museum pieces but as living, breathing, accessible culture.

Who it’s for

  • The Himachali living away from home who wants to reconnect.
  • The child growing up abroad who wants to know where they come from.
  • Anyone who deserves to know this culture exists, that it is rich, and that it matters.
Language is not only a tool for conversation. It is a tool for knowing yourself. And knowing yourself is where everything begins.

Pranjal Singh, Shimla & Mandi

Throughout history, many external powers and cultural forces passed through these hills, each leaving their mark, each changing something. But through all of it, something held. Our culture. Our traditions. Our language.

The people of Himachal carried what mattered most through every wave of history, not in books, but in their voices, their songs, their stories, their way of greeting a stranger and sitting with an elder.

Every winter vacation I visited my daadi in Mandi. There was always a barrier. I couldn’t fully understand what she said, and I didn’t try to. When you are young, culture doesn’t feel like something you could lose. It just feels like background. Like the mountains, always there, always will be.

Then at 15 I moved away from Himachal. And slowly, the way things do when distance enters, I started missing it. Not just the place, the people, the winters, the way my daadi’s house smelled, the sounds of a language I had never bothered to learn.

Now when I sit with my daadi and she tells me stories of her childhood, what life was like in her village, what the seasons meant, what the festivals felt like before roads connected everything, I am not just learning about her. I am learning about who I am and where I come from.

For the Himachali living away from home who wants to reconnect. For the child growing up abroad who wants to know where they come from. For anyone in the world who deserves to know that this culture exists, that it is rich, and that it matters.

Still here.
Still listening.
